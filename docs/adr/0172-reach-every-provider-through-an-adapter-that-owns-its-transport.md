# Reach every provider through an adapter that owns its transport

Google, the Channels, and the AI provider are each reached through one adapter that owns its URLs, its authentication, and its transport, and the automation is built from those three. The dependency bag that `createAutomation` accepted was not that: its Google member was a transport, `request(accessToken, url)`, so every caller still built Gmail, Calendar, and Drive URLs by hand; its attachment and token members were type aliases of functions that called the global `fetch` themselves; and the calendar invitation and the LINE push in `delivery.ts` were not in the bag at all. The seam had one adapter in production and none in tests, which is why seventy-nine tests stubbed `fetch` around it instead of substituting anything through it. One adapter is a hypothetical seam; a seam is real when a second adapter satisfies it.

The Google adapter speaks in the operations the product performs — read a message and its attachments, list history, send mail, upsert a Calendar event with `sendUpdates=none`, publish an attachment to Drive — and takes a Google Connection rather than an access token, so refreshing the token is its own concern and `invalid_grant` is reported once, where ADR 0124 needs it. Sending mail stays a Google operation rather than becoming a Channel, because ADR 0143 defines a Channel by an inbound endpoint and a handle namespace that email does not have. The LINE push moves entirely under the Channel seam, leaving `delivery.ts` as the Delivery Record module and nothing else. The AI adapter carries extraction, correspondence, and the agent turn. Each adapter has an in-memory twin under `apps/worker/test/` that states Gmail history, Calendar events, and sent messages as data, and a test reads and asserts on those rather than on request bodies.

## Consequences

`GoogleApiError` survives as the adapter's own error; the `AutomationDependencies` bag and `automation/providers.ts` do not. A test that wants to prove the exact HTTP request Google receives tests the production adapter directly, once, rather than every use-case through a stubbed `fetch`.
